OpenPulse Gen 2

Frequently asked questions

Short answers about OpenPulse Gen 2, the modular research wearable with swappable sensor pucks.

What is OpenPulse Gen 2?

OpenPulse Gen 2 is a modular research wearable from Munich, Germany. One core with two skin-side ports and one air-facing port takes swappable sensor pucks for PPG, skin temperature, ECG, EDA/bioimpedance and air quality. It records raw, timestamped data on the device and hands it to you locally as CSV, Parquet or EDF/BDF.

Can I buy OpenPulse Gen 2 today?

Not yet. Gen 2 is in development: it is designed and rendered from CAD, but not yet built or tested. If you are planning a study or pilot, tell us what you want to measure, on whom and for how long, and you get a proposal in writing.

Is OpenPulse a medical device?

No. OpenPulse is built for research and industrial use, not for diagnosis or treatment. The Air puck is not a certified gas detector or dosimeter.

Where is my data stored?

On the device first, then on your own computer: docked over USB, the device copies its raw data to your machine. There is no cloud and no account in between, and the raw data stays with you.

Which data formats and interfaces are there?

CSV, Parquet and EDF/BDF files, a Python library, a command-line tool, a local HTTP API and a local MCP server for AI assistants. The API is in development; see Data and API.

Which sensors are available?

Five sensor pucks: Pulse (raw PPG in green, red and infrared), Thermo+ (skin temperature and heat-flux trend), EKG (single-lead ECG), EDA / BioZ (skin conductance, respiration, bioimpedance) and Air (temperature, humidity, pressure, gas). All are in development with Gen 2. See Sensor pucks.

Can the pucks be swapped?

Yes, in the lab and by your team, not by the wearer. Four screws and standard tools: open the clear cover, lift the puck out, press the new one in. No soldering, no glue. Every port is mechanically keyed, so a puck cannot go in rotated or reversed.

Can you build a puck for a signal you do not cover?

Tell us what you need to measure. We check whether it fits a puck and build it for the same port and the same API.

What sample rates does OpenPulse record?

As specified for Gen 2: PPG at 100 Hz and 24 bit, motion at 50 Hz, skin temperature at 1 Hz and the Air puck every 3 seconds, each sample with its timestamp.

How long does the battery last and how much data fits on the device?

Design targets: a 50 mAh lithium cell for at least one day in study use, and 2 Gbit (256 MB) of onboard memory, about one day of raw data at full rate. These values are not yet measured.

Who is behind OpenPulse?

A student founding team based in Munich, Germany. See About.

Is OpenPulse related to OpenQASM OpenPulse or openpulse.org?

No. OpenPulse (openpulse.eu) is a modular research wearable. It is not related to the OpenQASM "OpenPulse" pulse grammar, to openpulse.org or to other projects with the same name.
